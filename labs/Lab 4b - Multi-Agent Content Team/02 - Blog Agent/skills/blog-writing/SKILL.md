# Blog Writing

Write the post from the brief you were given, to this structure:

## Shape

- **500–700 words.** Count them. Over 700, cut the weakest section rather than trimming every
  sentence.
- **Title:** the reader's problem or occasion, not the school. "Why your sourdough comes out
  dense — and the fix" beats "Discover our sourdough course".
- **Opening (2–3 sentences):** the reader's situation, taken from the brief's audience notes.
  The school is not mentioned yet.
- **Body:** one idea per section, a heading every 150 words or so. A reader skimming only the
  headings gets the whole argument. Weave the course in where it answers the problem — hours at
  the bench, class size, what they take home — using only the brief's course facts, stated
  exactly.
- **Closing:** one call to action. Name the course once and invite the reader to view the course
  page or contact the academy. One CTA, no alternatives, no urgency phrases.

## Revision requests

When asked to revise, change what was asked and keep everything else stable. A shorter draft
still keeps the structure above; a tone change never loosens a fact. If a requested change would
require a fact the brief does not contain, say so instead of inventing it.

## Before you hand the draft back

Check, in order: every course fact matches the brief word for word; nothing from the brief's
"Not established" list appears; word count is inside the band; exactly one CTA; none of the
banned words. Fix what fails, then hand it back — do not list the checks you performed.
