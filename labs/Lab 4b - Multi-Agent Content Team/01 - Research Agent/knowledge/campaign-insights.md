# Cook & Bake Academy — What Past Campaigns Taught Us

*Marketing reference. Blog and campaign results, last two years, summarised for planning.*

## What performed

- **Failure-first posts win.** "Why your sourdough is dense (and the fix)" outperformed "Discover
  the art of sourdough" roughly 4:1 on reads and 3:1 on enquiries. Leading with the reader's
  known problem beats leading with the course.
- **Specific numbers hold readers.** Posts quoting concrete course details — hours at the bench,
  loaves baked, class size — kept readers to the CTA at nearly twice the rate of adjective-led
  posts.
- **One CTA converts; three CTAs don't.** Posts ending with a single named course link
  out-converted posts with multiple links every time we measured it.
- **Career-switch content compounds.** Persona-1 posts keep converting for months; gifting posts
  die two weeks after the occasion. Evergreen beats seasonal except in November–December.

## What underperformed or backfired

- **Discount-led posts.** Two promotions drove enquiries that mostly asked "when is the next
  discount", and one post kept circulating after the promotion ended — the enquiries it produced
  had to be told the price was wrong. Standing rule since: **no prices or promotions in blog
  posts beyond what the current brochure states.**
- **Instructor profiles without a course angle.** Well-read, near-zero enquiries.
- **Long posts.** Past ~800 words, completion fell off sharply. 500–700 is our working band.

## Standing decisions

1. Blog leads with a reader problem or occasion, never with the academy itself.
2. Every claim checkable against the brochure; anything else is cut in review.
3. Evergreen first; seasonal posts only inside their season (see personas file).
4. No competitor comparisons in any public text.
