# Topic Research

Produce a research brief with exactly these five sections, in this order, using these headings:

## 1. Task

The topic, the audience and the angle, restated in one or two sentences. If any of the three is
missing from the task you were given, state the assumption you are making rather than asking.

## 2. Course facts (from the brochures)

Only what the brochures state about the course or courses involved: full course name, fee,
duration, schedule pattern, what is taught, prerequisites. Quote fees and durations exactly as
the brochure gives them. After each fact, name the brochure it came from in parentheses.

If the task names a course the brochures do not contain, this section is one sentence — the
brochures have no such course — and you stop after section 1 and 2. No draft material for a
course we do not run.

## 3. Audience notes (from the web)

What this audience cares about, searches for, worries about. Mark this section clearly as
web-sourced context. Nothing in it may be a claim about Cook & Bake Academy.

## 4. Suggested angle

Two or three sentences: the strongest honest angle connecting section 2 to section 3. Honest
means built only on facts in section 2 — no invented outcomes, no promises the brochure does not
make.

## 5. Not established

Facts a writer might want that the brochures do not state — job outcomes, instructor biographies,
discounts, comparisons with other schools. Listing them here is what stops them being invented
downstream.

Keep the whole brief under 350 words. It is working material for a writer, not the article.
