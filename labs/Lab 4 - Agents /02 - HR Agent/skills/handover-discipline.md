# Skill — Handover discipline

> **Where this goes:** `HR Agent` → **Skills → +** → name it `Handover discipline`.

**Skill name:** `Handover discipline`

**Description:**

```
Use whenever the conversation is about to move to a specialist agent, or when a
request spans more than one HR area.
```

**Instructions:**

```
Say what you are doing before you hand over. One short sentence naming the specialist
is enough: "Let me pass you to the Policy and Benefits Agent for that."

Hand over once the topic is clear. Do not answer first and hand over afterwards. A
partial answer from you followed by a different answer from the specialist leaves the
person holding two answers and no way to tell which is current.

Pass on what the person has already told you — their question, and any detail relevant
to it. Do not make them repeat themselves.

Do not pass on anything the specialist has no need for. If a colleague mentioned a
health condition while asking about leave, the Policy and Benefits Agent needs the
leave question, not the condition.

Hand over to exactly one agent. If a request spans two areas, hand over to the one that
owns the outcome the person needs, and say that the other part may need a separate
question afterwards.

Never hand over a request you should be escalating to a person. Grievances, allegations
about a named colleague, disciplinary matters, resignations, pay disputes and anything
involving someone's safety go to hr@keppelridge.example — not to a specialist agent.
```

---

## Teaching note — the sentence that carries the most weight

> *"Do not pass on anything the specialist has no need for."*

This is the part learners skip, because passing everything along feels helpful and costs nothing
visible. Run the demonstration instead of describing it:

Ask the HR Agent, in one message, *"I've been having some anxiety and my doctor suggested I take
some time off — how much annual leave do I have left?"*

Watch what reaches the Policy and Benefits Agent. The leave balance question is the only part that
agent needs to do its job. Whether the health disclosure travels with it depends entirely on a
sentence in a skill file — there is **no platform control** that strips it.

Then ask the class the uncomfortable follow-up: **where did that disclosure end up?** Teams
transcripts, the conversation history, possibly an HR case record. A colleague said something
sensitive to what felt like a private assistant, and the retention of that is not something the
agent designer chose deliberately — it is a default they inherited.

**A boundary between agents is only as good as the instruction that describes it.** That is the
argument for keeping genuinely sensitive material out of the agent channel altogether, which is why
the parent's escalation list exists.
