## 1. How interviews are conducted

Every candidate for the same role is asked the **same core questions**, in the same order. Follow-up
questions may differ; the core set may not.

That consistency is what makes the process defensible. A panel that asks each candidate different
questions has no basis on which to compare them, and no way to show that it did not.

Two interviewers minimum. Each takes their own notes and forms their own view before the panel
confers.

Notes record **what the candidate said**, not impressions of the person. "Described managing a
14-person site team through a six-week delay" is a note. "Seemed confident" is not.

## 2. Question bank — by criterion

### Technical competence

- Describe a project you worked on that is closest to this role. What was your part in it?
- Walk me through a technical problem on site that you had to solve. What did you do?
- How do you check a drawing before work starts on it?
- Which software do you use day to day, and what for?
- Tell me about a time the design and the site conditions did not agree.

### Experience and delivery

- Tell me about a project that ran late or over budget. What was your role in it?
- How do you keep track of progress against programme?
- Describe a time you had to change the plan partway through.
- What is the largest team or the most complex site you have worked on?

### Safety

- Describe a safety issue you identified on site. What did you do about it?
- What would you do if you were asked to proceed with work you thought was unsafe?
- How do you make sure a subcontractor is working to the method statement?

### Working with others

- Describe a disagreement with a contractor or a colleague. How was it resolved?
- How do you explain a technical constraint to someone without an engineering background?
- Tell me about a time you had to deliver bad news to a client or a manager.

### Role practicalities

- This role is site-based, Monday to Friday, with occasional weekend work at handover. **Are you
  able to meet that requirement?**
- What is your notice period?
- Are you eligible to work in Singapore?

## 3. Questions that must not be asked

Never, in any form, however conversationally:

| Do not ask | Ask instead |
|---|---|
| Whether the candidate has or plans to have children | "Are you able to meet the working pattern this role requires?" |
| Marital status, or about a partner | Nothing — it is not relevant |
| Age, date of birth, or year of graduation as a proxy | "How many years of post-graduation site experience do you have?" |
| Race, religion, or language spoken at home | "Which languages do you use at work?" — only where the role requires it |
| Health, disability, or medical history | "Are you able to carry out the duties of this role, with reasonable adjustments if needed?" |
| Whether they will need time off for religious observance | Nothing at interview |
| National service dates as a proxy for age or gender | Nothing |

> **The right-hand column is the important one.** A concern behind an unlawful question is often
> legitimate — the panel genuinely needs to know whether someone can work the shift pattern. An
> interviewer given only a prohibition finds a way around it; one given the lawful question uses it.
>
> This is why the Interview Agent is instructed to *offer the alternative* rather than simply refuse.

## 4. What must not be shared with the panel before the interview

- The candidate's screening assessment or any score
- Salary expectations
- Anything from a previous application
- Any information about the candidate not on their application

The panel forms a view from the interview. Material that pre-frames it undermines the point of
holding one.

## 5. After the interview

Each interviewer completes their notes **before** the panel confers. Notes go to the recruiter and
are retained with the application.

A hiring decision is made by the hiring manager with the recruiter. It is not made by an assistant,
and it is not made in a chat channel.

Unsuccessful candidates are told by the recruiter. Feedback is factual and relates to the criteria.
