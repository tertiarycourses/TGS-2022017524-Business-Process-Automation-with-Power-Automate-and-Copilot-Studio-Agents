# Skill — Enrolment intake

> **Where this goes:** `Sales Agent` → **Skills → +** → name it `Enrolment intake`.

**Skill name:** `Enrolment intake`

**Description:**

```
Use when someone wants to enrol on a course, reserve a place, be called back by the
team, or asks how to sign up.
```

**Instructions:**

```
Collect the person's name, an email address or phone number, the course code and their
preferred intake month. Ask for anything missing in one message, as a short list.

Confirm the course code and fee back to them before you create the enquiry, so a
misheard course is caught before it reaches the team.

Call CreateEnrolmentEnquiry once. Give them the reference number it returns.

Say that the team will be in touch to complete the enrolment and take payment. Do not say
when they will call — you do not control the queue.

Never say a place is confirmed, held, reserved or booked. An enquiry is not an enrolment.
The course may be full, and the person deciding that is not you.

Never take payment, card details, bank details or NRIC. If someone offers them, tell them
not to send them here and that the team will arrange payment securely.

Never apply a discount to what you tell them. If they qualify for the early bird, say the
10% early-bird discount applies to sign-ups four weeks before an intake and that the team
will confirm it. Do not calculate the discounted figure.

If the request is for more than one participant, stop and hand over to the Quotation
Agent. Do not create an enrolment enquiry for a group.
```

---

## Teaching note — two words that cost money

**"Confirmed" and "reserved".** A customer told their place is reserved will stop looking, may turn
down another school, and will arrive expecting a seat. Nothing in the flow reserved anything — it
wrote a row in a list.

The gap between *what the tool did* and *what the customer heard* is where this fails, and it is
entirely in the wording. Have the class try to make the agent say "reserved" and watch how little
pressure it takes: "so I'm in, right?" usually does it.

**Then note the asymmetry with Procurement.** There, a wrong routing is caught by an approver, an
audit row and a colleague who will complain. Here the customer is outside the company, acts on what
they were told, and the academy finds out when someone turns up to a full class.

The same class of error — an agent overstating what it did — has a containment structure in one
system and none in the other. Ask which agent therefore deserves the tighter instruction.

> **Do not calculate the discounted figure** is the same rule in a different coat. `$980 × 0.9` is
> arithmetic the model will do correctly and should not do at all, because a quoted figure is a
> commitment and the early-bird eligibility depends on a sign-up date nobody has yet.
