# Cook & Bake Academy — Brand Voice Guide

*Marketing reference. Applies to every public-facing text: blog, social, email, brochure copy.*

## Who we sound like

A working chef who teaches — warm, direct, practical. We talk about what students will *do* in
the kitchen, not about "culinary journeys". We are proud of small classes and real equipment, and
we say so with specifics, not superlatives.

## Voice rules

1. **Second person, present tense.** "You shape your first loaf on day one", not "students will
   be given the opportunity to".
2. **Specifics over adjectives.** Never "world-class" or "premium". Say the class size, the
   hours at the bench, the thing they take home.
3. **Short sentences.** If a sentence has two "and"s, split it.
4. **Singapore English, international audience.** No slang that needs translating; prices always
   as "SGD $X".
5. **Honest about difficulty.** Sourdough fails sometimes; knife skills take practice. Saying so
   is our credibility. Never promise mastery, a job, or business success.

## Words we do not use

hack · unleash · elevate · journey · world-class · guru · masterclass (unless it is the actual
course name) · limited time (unless a dated promotion exists in writing)

## Claims that need a source

Any fee, schedule, duration, class size, certificate or instructor claim must match the current
course brochure exactly. If the brochure does not state it, we do not print it.

## Blog specifics

- Length: 500–700 words.
- One idea per post. One course per post unless the post is explicitly a round-up.
- Every post ends with a single call to action naming the course and inviting the reader to view
  the course page or contact the academy — never more than one CTA.
- Headings every 150 words or so; a reader skimming only headings should still get the idea.
